## Support T32 and Institutional Training Programs

### Extend your training environment with BDC
BDC can complement the scientific and methodological training provided by T32 and other institutional
programs with hands-on experience using biomedical data, cloud-based analysis environments, and
reproducible computational workflows.

Programs can incorporate BDC into an existing curriculum or use it for a focused learning experience.

BDC may be a fit for:
- NIH T32 programs
- graduate and postdoctoral training programs
- early-career investigator initiatives
- summer research programs
- cohort-based training
- other institutional research training programs

### Give trainees experience beyond the classroom
Trainees can gain practical experience with large-scale biomedical data, cloud-based analysis
environments, reproducible computational workflows, secondary data analysis, and collaborative
research.
<Link to="/help/contact-bdc" class="text-bold">Discuss your training program</Link>.

## Courses & Workshops
Bring cloud-based biomedical research into graduate or undergraduate courses, workshops, short modules,
or other group learning experiences.

BDC has supported instruction in cloud computing, genome-wide association studies, RNA-seq and genomic
analysis, collaborative data analysis, and reproducible research workflows.
<Link to="/help/contact-bdc" class="text-bold">Discuss Teaching with BDC</Link>.

## Plan an experience that fits your program
You do not need to start with a fully designed BDC curriculum. Start with your learning goals and
timeframe, and our team can help determine how BDC can fit into the experience.

1. **Tell us what you are planning**

    Share your program, audience, learning objectives, approximate number of participants, and timeline.
1. **Identify the right BDC experience**

    Determine which BDC resources, platforms, tools, or learning activities align with your goals.
1. **Prepare for the training**

    Plan participant access, technical requirements, cloud costs, and instructional needs in advance.
1. **Put BDC into practice**

    Use BDC as part of a course, workshop, training module, cohort experience, or research activity.

**Planning an academic course?** Contact BDC early; activities requiring coordinated
technical or instructional support should generally begin planning at least one semester in advance.

### Summer Institute in Statistical Genetics (SISG)
BDC-Seven Bridges has been used in the [Bruce Weir Summer Institute in Statistical Genetics (SISG)](https://sisg.biosciences.gatech.edu/)
Whole Genome Sequence Analysis Pipeline module to support hands-on instruction in cloud-based
whole-genome sequence analysis workflows. Students learn how cloud ecosystems can support large-scale
data analysis in a practical instructional setting.

## Supporting Early Career Investigators
BDC collaborates with fellowship and training programs that introduce early-career investigators to
cloud-based biomedical research. These collaborations provide opportunities to gain experience using BDC
resources while advancing scientific research.

Selected fellowship-related collaborations and references are listed below.

### NHLBI BDC Fellows Program

From March 2020 through February 2022, the NHLBI BDC Fellows Program supported more than 45
early-career researchers. Fellows advanced scientific research using BDC and provided feedback that
helped inform development of the BDC ecosystem. Their work also contributed to some of the earliest
BDC-enabled research.

<Button to="/about/bdc/fellows" variant="outline">View the NHLBI BDC Fellows</Button>

### TOPMed Fellowship Program
The [NHLBI Trans-Omics for Precision Medicine](https://topmed.nhlbi.nih.gov/) (TOPMed) Fellowship Program
provides training to early-career investigators, leveraging the TOPMed datasets and the BDC computing
environment to conduct impactful precision medicine research.

### AIM-AHEAD Research Fellowship
The [AIM-AHEAD Research Fellowship](https://www.aim-ahead.net/programs/research-fellowship-program/)
names BDC as a "dataset option" early-career researcher applicants from under-represented populations
can use to actively participate in biomedical research that involves the use of artificial
intelligence/machine learning methodologies.
